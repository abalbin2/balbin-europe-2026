Valencia

📅 May 8 – 10  ·  3 nights

Your trip begins in Valencia — Tony & Sarah's home. Spain's third-largest city sits on the Mediterranean coast and is famous for its futuristic architecture, incredible food scene (the original home of paella), and year-round sunshine. Take it easy, shake off the jet lag, and enjoy the city before the Italy adventure begins.

Day Plans
May 20 — Wednesday
Cinque Terre: Vernazza + Manarola

Leave early — CT gets packed by 10:30am. Buy the Cinque Terre Train Card (day pass, unlimited trains between villages) at the SML station before you board.

  • 8:45
    Leave the AirbnbHead out together
  • 8:50
    Coffee + pastry near the stationStanding, Italian-style — 10 minutes max. Don't sit down.
  • 9:05
    SML station — buy CT Train CardsOne each at the ticket window. Day pass, unlimited trains between all 5 villages.
  • 9:20
    Train departs toward La Spezia~50 min to Vernazza. The CT villages are on this line.
Vernazza — ~2 hours
  • 10:10
    Arrive Vernazza — walk down Via RomaExit the station, walk straight toward the sea. 3 minutes, leads directly to the harbor.
  • Piazza Marconi — the harbor squareStop here. This is the money shot: colored buildings, small harbor, castle above. Take it in.
  • Walk the harbor breakwaterThe stone pier jutting into the sea — best angle back at the village.
  • Climb to Castello DoriaStairs on the east side of the harbor. ~200 steps, 10 min. €1.50 entry. Best panorama of the whole trip from the top.
  • Coffee or gelato at the harborSit at one of the harbor cafés — rest your legs, the view from a table here is excellent.
  • 12:15
    Back to stationWander up Via Roma, browse the shops, back to the station for the train to Manarola.
Manarola — ~2.5 hours
  • 12:40
    Arrive Manarola — exit through the tunnelYou emerge onto Via Discovolo, the main village street.
  • Turn left — walk uphill through the village firstGo up while you have energy. Colorful houses, much quieter than Vernazza.
  • Punta Bonfiglio viewpointFollow signs at the top of the village. Small headland with a terrace — look back at the whole village stacked on the cliff. This is the iconic Manarola shot.
  • Walk back down to the harbor rocksSteps lead to the natural port — fishing boats, flat rocks by the water. Rest here 10–15 min.
  • 13:15
    Lunch on Via DiscovoloSeveral solid restaurants on the main street. Sit down, take a proper break.
  • 14:30
    Final wander, then head to stationOne last slow walk back through the village toward the station.
  • 15:30
    Train back to SMLVia La Spezia — ~1 hour. Back in SML around 16:30. Aperitivo on the waterfront if anyone has legs left.
May 21 — Thursday
Portofino + San Fruttuoso

Take the morning ferry from SML to Portofino (~10 min, sheltered coastal crossing — low seasick risk). If energy allows, continue by boat to San Fruttuoso — a medieval abbey in a secluded car-free bay with almost no crowds.

  • Morning
    Ferry SML → Portofino (~10 min)Take the boat, not the bus — the sea approach gives the best first view of the harbor.
  • Walk the Portofino harborThe piazza, pastel buildings, yachts. Very photogenic, very polished. Best view in Europe of its kind.
  • Walk up to the church (San Giorgio)Above the piazza — panoramic view back over the harbor. 10 min climb, worth it.
  • If energy
    Boat Portofino → San Fruttuoso (~20 min)Medieval abbey sitting alone in a wild, car-free cove. Almost no crowds — boat-only access. Lunch here, explore the abbey, sit by the water. The most memorable spot of the whole Riviera leg.
  • Boat back to SMLDirect from San Fruttuoso, or via Portofino.
If tired — Portofino only
Skip San Fruttuoso. Ferry to Portofino, 2 hours, ferry back. Relaxed afternoon in SML.
If seasick — no boats
Portofino by bus (10 min from SML) + Camogli by train (15 min). Colorful fishing village, no boats needed.

Lake Como
Azzano / Mezzegra, Lombardy
📅 May 22 – 25  ·  3 nights

Lake Como is one of Italy's most beautiful places — dramatic Alps dropping straight into deep blue water, dotted with pastel villages and grand 19th-century villas. You're staying in the quiet hamlet of Azzano, above Lenno, at a private villa on the lake's quieter western shore. Villa Carlotta — one of Italy's finest botanical gardens, at peak azalea and rhododendron season in May — is a 5-minute bus ride away.
